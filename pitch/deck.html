01 · The problem

Every small business already knows what went wrong

The camera fires. The dashboard shows the return rate. The ad tool says "winner." The POS has last week's sales. Nobody tells the one person standing there what to do next.

Returns
A seller above 20% returns reads every one by hand, and phones buyers before they order to say which size to keep.
Prep
A shift lead guesses how much to make from memory, every single morning, with no time to check anything else.
Ads
An owner pays an agency and can't tell what it bought, or trusts a "winner" the sample size can't actually support.
Theft
A $10–20 item walks out with no one in the shop knowing what to do in the ten seconds after the alert.
the "reason" field customers pick at checkout is useless… neither tells me if it's the product, the photos, or the sizing.r/AmazonSeller, a seller above 20% returns
02 · The insight

The real competitor is the habit, not the incumbent tool

Existing tools were built for a business with more volume, more budget or more staff than the one that has the problem.

Tool classBuilt forWhat the small business lacks
Sizing tools (True Fit, Bold Metrics)Brand storefronts, ~$1,650/mo entryCannot be installed on a marketplace listing
Marketing agencies~$3,500/mo, accounts big enough to testEnough conversions for a clean A/B test
Performance Max30–50+ conversions a monthThe volume that keeps the automation stable
AI video analyticsMulti-staff stores, $8–35K installedA second person to act on the alert

So each module is scored against the habit it replaces, not against a vendor — Prep must beat "same weekday last week" or it doesn't ship.

04 · How it's built

No product prompt was written by hand

A meta-prompt writes every version. A team of seven agents builds, tests, and scores it. It ships only when it beats the version it replaces — on real data, not just a clean test set.

05 · Evidence, reported honestly

Six prompt versions. Three were rejected on purpose.

v4 regressed on real data. v5 broke evidence grounding. v6 failed once on a single malformed-JSON output — which turned out to be a bug in our own eval runner, not the prompt. Fixing that bug let the exact same v6 prompt pass cleanly.
