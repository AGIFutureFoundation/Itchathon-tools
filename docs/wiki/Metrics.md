# Metrics: the whole evals story

*Powered by AGI Corp*

This page is the one-stop version of Sapient.X's evals for anyone who won't read every module page: what "gated" means, what actually shipped, what got rejected, and why.

## 1. What "gated" means

Every product prompt in Sapient.X is generated by a meta-prompt, never hand-written, and none of them ships to a user until it beats a measured baseline: a synthetic gate (cause accuracy ≥ 80%, evidence grounded ≥ 95%, low-data honesty ≥ 99%) and, for Returns, a real-data check on 30 real Amazon-review cases. A version can pass the synthetic gate and still be blocked — the release rule is that it must not regress either gate, and real-world performance is checked every time, not assumed from the synthetic score.

## 2. The whole story in one chart

![Synthetic vs real-30 accuracy: v2 10.0%, v3 56.7%, v4 46.7%](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AGIFutureFoundation/Itchathon-tools/main/media/charts/synthetic-vs-real.png)

Synthetic cases score much higher than real ones because real Amazon listings are messier than the golden set: they often lack a size chart entirely, and review volume is far lower and noisier than the synthetic cases assume. That gap — synthetic 98% vs. real 40% on the first real test — is reported as the project's headline honest finding, not smoothed over or hidden from the numbers shown here.

## 4. What went wrong, and how we know

![v2 confusion matrix](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AGIFutureFoundation/Itchathon-tools/main/media/charts/confusion-v2.png)
![v3 confusion matrix](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AGIFutureFoundation/Itchathon-tools/main/media/charts/confusion-v3.png)

v3's real-30 failure report surfaced a garment-vs-fulfilment confusion pattern: a `garment` cause (a quality failure discovered through use) getting mixed up with `fulfilment` (a shipping/logistics failure discovered at unboxing). v5 was a hand-written targeted attempt to fix exactly that confusion, and it did raise synthetic accuracy to 95% — but the fix cost it evidence grounding, which fell below the gate, so v5 never even reached a real-data test.

## 5. Beyond accuracy: does a human agree, and would the owner use it

**Judge calibration:** on a stratified 15-case sample spanning all six cause labels, drawn from v3's synthetic outputs, a `claude -p` judge scored each output blind to the human-proxy score using judge.md's rubric. Result: **13/15 agreed within one point (86.7%)**, against a gate of ≥ 12/15 — passed.

**Persona-Ben acceptance:** the same 15 outputs were shown, blind to the prompt and the raw case data, to a role-played solo seller (Ben) who answered would-use-tomorrow yes/no. Result: **13/15 said yes (86.7%)**, against a gate of ≥ 70% — passed.

## 6. Prep and Ads

![Naive vs model pinball loss backtest](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AGIFutureFoundation/Itchathon-tools/main/media/charts/prep-backtest.png)

Prep beat the "same weekday last week" habit by **+30.4%** on pinball loss (run-out cases +32.6%, neutral cases +28.0%), against a ≥15% gate.

Ads passed its stats-honesty gate **20/20** on `test_is_valid` (`evals/ads/cases.jsonl`, `evals/ads/results.json`). Case `a01` is the hackathon example: Headline A at 135 impressions/0 conversions vs Headline B at 122/2 — a plain calculator would call B the winner, but the module correctly returns `test_is_valid: false` and "no winner yet".
